# pagefit: rules

Rules PF01-PF28 of `lib/hwpx_docs/pagefit/`. Evidence keys: M = measured against Hangul 2024 pages (`hd render hwpx`, the text bottom of each page
read from the page image), T = `hd selftest --only pagefit`.

| id | rule | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| PF01 | The page model: heights of text lines and table rows come from the preview through the registered backend `blocks` / `preview` (`preview/blocks.py`); the pages come from this module's own paginator (PF02, measured against Hangul) or from the preview's flow. The flow (`registry.backend('pagereport', 'preview')`, `preview/report.py`) applies keep_next, keep_lines, widow and page_break_before since preview rule P17 (`page_report.keep_rules` True), but not the way Hangul does in every case (measured on 6 canary documents (`model._canaries`): a keep_next that Hangul leaves alone, `page_break: none`, merged rows, a repeated header, a paragraph that keeps with a table, a table that keeps with the next paragraph). So `model.renderer_matches()` runs the canaries through both once per run and only when the flow gives the pages of the own paginator within 1.5 mm every section is read from the flow (what the preview owner asked for); until then only the sections that need the flow (columns, footnotes, objects) come from it and every other section from the own paginator. The flow applies no keep rule inside table cells, text boxes, headers and footers, nor in the area after a column change (preview rules P17); pagefit neither moves nor checks those. A line fits when `y + h - trail <= bottom` (the spacing under the last line is not needed at the page end). With every rule off the page count is the preview's: 267 of 267 single-column fixtures. | T; M |
| PF02 | Keep rules as Hangul applies them (`hh:breakSetting`), measured in 17 test documents (the text bottom of every page within 1 mm): the page is cut line by line and a cut Hangul would not make moves up until it is allowed. `page_break` / `page_break_before` start a page unless the block is already first on one. `keep_next`: when a page would end between a block and the next PARAGRAPH, the part of the block that is on that page goes to the next page too (a whole one-line heading; the whole of a multi-line paragraph; NOT only its last line; nothing happens when the paragraph is already cut so that its last line is with the next one); toward a table or a picture `keep_next` does nothing. `keep_lines` keeps a paragraph on one page when it fits on one. `widow` leaves no single line at the bottom of a page (the whole paragraph goes on) and none at the top. A table is cut between rows only (`page_break` `table`), not inside a group of merged rows (`rs`), not at all with `page_break: none` when it fits a page; with `page_break: cell` (the builder default) Hangul may cut inside a cell. The header rows (`header_rows`, `repeat_header`) come again at the top of the next page. | M: 6 plan documents (6 -> 4 pages, 5 -> 4, ...), keep_next at 14 page positions, chains, tables t1-t6 |
| PF03 | Findings of `check`: `empty` (a page except the last is filled less than `--fill` percent, default 85; with the free space in mm and why the next page starts: forced break, keep rule, split), `heading` (the last block of a page is a heading and its paragraph is on the next page), `orphan_end` (one line of a paragraph at the bottom), `orphan_start` (one line carried over to the top), `table` (a table cut after its header row(s), `header_rows`), `last` (the last page has `--last-lines` lines or rows or fewer, default 3). Exit code 1 when something is found. A section with columns is skipped. | T |
| PF04 | A heading: an `outline_level`, `keep_next` set, a style whose name starts with 개요/제목/Heading/Title, or short (60 characters) bold text at least 1.5 pt bigger than the body text (the size with most text). | T |
| PF05 | A section with more than one column, a column change, footnotes or endnotes, or objects that text goes around (shapes, a picture or object that is not `inline`, a table with `pos`, `same_para`) is laid out by the preview's own flow, not by this module's paginator (`model.uses_flow`; PF13). A vertical-writing section is skipped (`Page.skipped`, the check says so). A spec with `class` or `named_styles` is resolved for the layout only (`core.layout.Layout.eff`). | T |
| PF06 | Input: `.hwpx` (read with `core.read`, so the blocks are Hangul's paragraphs) or `.json` spec (the blocks as written; a `p` text with `\n` is one paragraph with line breaks here and several paragraphs in the builder). Output of `fix`: `.json` (the fixed spec) or `.hwpx` (built from it). | T |
| PF07 | `fix` step 1: every heading gets `keep_next`, every other text paragraph of two lines or more gets `widow`. | M; T |
| PF08 | `fix` step 2 and 3: a table cut after its header row(s) gets `page_break_before`; a forced break is taken away when the page before it is filled less than `--break-fill` (default 70) percent, or less than `--fill` when the spacing cannot take the free space (`max-extra` x the paragraphs on the page), or when it follows a heading that would stand alone at the bottom of its page. Decided one break at a time, the document is laid out again after each. | M: plan 6 -> 4 pages; T |
| PF09 | `fix` step 4: a last page of `--last-lines` lines or fewer (not after a forced break) is pulled back by shrinking `after` of the paragraphs of the page before it by at most `--max-shrink` pt each (default 3, never below 0), only when the page count then drops. | M: 2 pages -> 1 page in Hangul; T |
| PF10 | `fix` step 5: the free space under the text of every other page (more than 3 mm) is spread over `after` of the paragraphs that end on that page (not the last one), at most `--max-extra` pt each (default 6); tried at 100, 80, 60, 40, 25 and 10 percent of the amount until no text moves to the next page and no page count changes. A page that cannot take all keeps the rest: the check after the fix says so. Text size, line spacing, margins never change. | M: pages 100 % |
| PF11 | Allowed changes (T checks it): the keys `keep_next`, `widow`, `page_break`, `page_break_before`, `after`; no `size`. | T |
| PF12 | After the fix the check runs again and `hd pagefit fix` prints before, the changes and after. | T |
| PF13 | Pages from the preview's flow (`registry.backend('pagereport', 'preview')`, `preview/report.py`): the flow's own `put` records which atom went to which page and column (`flow.lay_out(spec, dir, Trace())`; the section is laid out twice sometimes, only the last layout counts), the footnote blocks are added, `used` = the text bottoms of the columns (spacing under the last line not counted) + the footnote blocks, `fill` = used / (columns x body height). `items` are the rows of each block per page, counted over the document, so findings and fixes read both kinds of pages the same way. Since the preview applies the keep rules (`page_report.keep_rules` True) its pages are read as they are; a flow without them (False) gives pages without the rules, and findings that the rules solve (a heading alone, a single line) are not reported when the block has the flag, because Hangul applies it. Measured: preview and Hangul agree on the page bottom, the 3 footnote lines and the separator within 0.3 mm (page of 12 paragraphs with footnotes), and on the page count of a 2-column plan (5 pages -> 4 after the fix), a plan with footnotes (6 -> 4) and with square-wrap objects (6 -> 4). | M; T |
| PF14 | Step 5 on a page from the preview's flow leaves `--safety-flow` mm (default 8) plus 4 mm for each footnote on the page free under the text, other pages `--safety` mm (default 1.5). Measured: the same plan with footnotes, filled to 8 mm, spilled two lines of a paragraph to a new page in Hangul (5 pages, the page count the check had predicted 4); with 8 + 4 mm per footnote it was 4 in Hangul. The pages without keep rules in the preview and the Hangul rules (widow) are the likely cause: the margin is empirical. | M |
| PF15 | Reading units (`units.py`): the groups of blocks a reader should not have to turn a page for. Strong, kept whole when they fit on a page: `heading` (headings in a row + the first content block under them: a table, picture or box whole, a paragraph of 8 lines or fewer), `item` (a block with an item mark and the deeper ones after it, notes `※` included; a group of more than 80 percent of a page is kept as item + first sub item), `table` / `figure` (with its caption `표 1.`, the sentence that introduces it `아래 표와 같이`, `다음과 같`, a sentence ending in `:`, its unit line `(단위: 원)` and the notes under it `※`, `주)`, `자료:`, `출처:`), `box` (bordered or filled paragraphs in a row), `attach` (`붙임` and its numbered lines up to `끝.`), `tail` (the last paragraph, `끝.` and what follows: 발신 명의, 결재란). Weak: `section` (a heading with all up to the next heading of its level or higher, when at most `--section-ratio` = 0.4 of a page) and `list` (a run of numbered items of one level, at most `--list-ratio` = 0.5 of a page). Units that share a block are one unit when the whole fits a page. Item marks: 개조식 `□ ○ - ·` and the 공문 levels L1-L8 of gongmun-style (`1.` `가.` `1)` `가)` `(1)` `(가)` `①` `㉮`), or a `number` key. | T; M |
| PF16 | A unit is held by flags when it is paragraphs only: `keep_next` on every paragraph of it but the last, `keep_lines` on each, `page_break: none` on a table that fits (`units.wanted_flags`). Hangul ties a paragraph to the next PARAGRAPH only (PF02), so a unit with a table or picture after a paragraph (introduction + table) is held by `page_break_before` on its first block, added only when the unit would be cut (a break is part of the layout then; PF19 does not take it away). A break goes before the first block of the unit that holds the block, and of every unit that fits a page and holds that block. | M: intro + table `none` (Hangul: the intro stays, no tie); break before the intro (Hangul: both on the next page) |
| PF17 | Tables longer than a page: `page_break: table`, `repeat_header` on when the table has header rows; neither the first nor the last part has fewer than `--min-rows` (3) rows: a short first part starts the table on a new page, a short last part gets room above the table (its top margin grows by the missing rows) so the cut moves up; the check says what is left. Row heights of a table without `heights` are what the builder writes (lines x size x line spacing + cell margins, 6.64 mm per row at 10 pt and 160 percent); `model.resolved` puts them in for the preview. | M: 70-row table, 3 pages, rows per page and the repeated header as predicted (page bottoms within 1 mm) |
| PF18 | `check` kinds of the second version: `unit`, `table_rows`, `table_head` (a table over pages without header rows or with `repeat_header` off), `table_cell` (`page_break: cell` on a table over pages), `ref` (a sentence that points at `표 N` / `그림 N` is on another page than that table or figure; the caption is the block next to the table or picture), `note` (a footnote goes on at the next page: pages of the preview flow), `pageref` (the text names a page: `3쪽`, `다음 쪽`, `앞 쪽`: only a reminder, fixing may move it). Unit findings on pages of the preview flow are not reported when the flags of PF16 are all there (Hangul applies them, the preview does not). | T |
| PF19 | Step order of `fix` (fix.py): flags of headings and paragraphs; strong units; long tables; forced breaks after an empty page (and one right after a lonely heading, and one whose unit would fit in the free space above it); breaks before units that flags cannot hold; footnote continuation (the paragraph with the last note of the page starts the next page, kept when the note findings drop and the pages grow by one at most); table parts (PF17); the short last page; weak units, one at a time, kept only when no page gets emptier and the page count does not grow; the spacing under paragraphs (`after`) and tables (outer margin below) up to `--max-extra` pt each. Never changed: text, order, size. | T; M |
| PF20 | A page that stays empty after all that (a unit that does not fit the free space moved to the next page) is reported by the check with its free space; the order of the content is not changed to fill it. | T |
| PF21 | Other situations that make a reader turn pages, and what pagefit does with them (the list of the report): a heading alone at the bottom (fixed: keep_next); one line of a paragraph alone at the top or bottom (fixed: widow); an item cut from its sub items (fixed); a table cut anywhere (fixed: held whole, or cut between rows with a repeated header and 3 rows at least); a table cut after its header (fixed); a table without header rows cut over pages (reported); a cell cut in the middle (fixed: `page_break: table`); a caption, introduction or unit line on another page than the table (fixed); a summary box cut (fixed); the last paragraph, `끝.` and the 결문 split (fixed); 붙임 lines split (fixed); a footnote going on at the next page (fixed when moving the paragraph helps, else reported); a sentence pointing at `표 N` on another page (reported); a pointer to a page number or direction (reported); a short section or a numbered list cut (weak units, fixed when no page gets emptier); a short last page (fixed when it can be pulled back, else reported); an empty page (reported). | T |
| PF22 | Profiles (`profile.py`, `profiles/*.json`): the readability rules are a named style, not the default. `hd pagefit check|fix --profile NAME|PATH`; without one the profile is `minimal` (the first version: the least change); `readable` is the full set; a user profile is a JSON file with the same keys, `"extends": "readable"` (or another profile or file) starts from it and only the keys written change. Keys: `check.kinds` (finding kinds reported), `check.fill` (percent a page except the last should be filled, 85), `check.last_lines`; `fix.keep.headings|widow`; `fix.units.strong` (heading, item, table, figure, box, attach, tail), `fix.units.weak` (section, list), `section_ratio` 0.4, `list_ratio` 0.5, `item_ratio` 0.8, `heading_lines` 8; `fix.tables.cut_between_rows|repeat_header|min_rows|header_only_fix|split_gap_mm|split_min_ratio|free_heights` (PF24); `fix.breaks.remove_after_empty|break_fill|heading_before_break|pull_up|unit_break|chain_start|chain_max` (PF23); `fix.notes.move_paragraph`; `fix.last_page.pull_back|last_lines|max_shrink`; `fix.spacing.fill|tables|max_extra|safety|safety_flow`. A wrong key or value stops with a message that names it. The command line options (`--fill`, `--min-rows`, `--max-extra` ...) override single values. `hd pagefit profiles [NAME]` lists the profiles or prints one in full. The skill `hwpx-readability` chooses a profile when the user asks for it. | T |
| PF23 | A forced break goes before the whole keep chain, not before its last block. Hangul does not carry a block that has `keep_next` over a forced break (`page_break_before`) on the next block: the heading (or the introduction) stays alone at the page end, or Hangul moves it to a page of its own and the break makes one more (measured on 7 documents: 20 headings alone). So every break that `fix` places or finds before a block that follows a `keep_next` chain (at most `breaks.chain_max` 6 blocks; a block with a break of its own ends the chain) moves to the first block of the chain (`fix._move_break`, `_chain_pass`; on by `breaks.chain_start`, in `minimal` and `readable`). `check` reports a `heading` finding for a block that has `keep_next`, ends a page and is followed by a forced break. A heading box (a table of one cell with a short bold text above body size) is a heading (`model.is_heading`); a heading box before a table is not tied by any flag (Hangul carries a table with `keep_next` only to a PARAGRAPH, measured with a 2 x 2 table: the whole table goes), so the unit needs a break before the box, or the table is cut between rows (PF24). | T; M |
| PF24 | A big table is cut between rows instead of leaving a big gap (profile `readable`: `tables.split_gap_mm` 40, `split_min_ratio` 0.33; `split_gap_mm` 0 = off). When a table of a third of a page or more fits a page but not the space under the text before it, moving it whole (`page_break: none`, or the unit with its heading) leaves more than `split_gap_mm` mm empty on the page before: only that table gets `page_break: table` and repeating header rows (the other tables keep `none`); `fix` keeps the change when no finding that a reader feels (unit, table_rows = a part of fewer than `min_rows` rows, table cut after the header, heading, single line) is added. The same holds for a table that fits a page alone but not with the heading that keeps with it (Hangul does not carry a heading to a table): it is cut between rows so that the heading stands with its first rows. `check` does not report such a cut unit when the table has `page_break: table`, repeats its header, and the blocks of the unit before the table are on the page where the table starts and those after it on the page where it ends. `tables.free_heights` (readable: false) deletes fixed row heights of a table that is cut: Hangul moved a long table with repeated header rows and the row heights of `hd html` whole (measured), but the builder's own row heights are smaller than Hangul's text (measured: text lines overlapped in a long table), so the heights stay by default and only a profile that knows its table text fits may free them. A table that is not cut because a unit does not fit by a few mm (the notes under it go on) stays whole and the gap is reported. | T; M |
| PF25 | Pages of a document with footnotes (or columns, objects) come from the preview's flow (PF13): checked on 7 real planning documents made from HTML (11 footnotes each): the model's page count equals Hangul's on all 7. The flow does not carry a table with `keep_next` at the page end to the next page as Hangul does (a heading box moves with its paragraph, measured; canary 6 of `model._canaries`); so `model.pages` runs the flow again with a break before such a table (at most 8 times, `_carried`) while `_table_carry_ok()` is false, and the page that starts there has the reason `keep_next`. The flow also applies no keep rule inside cells, text boxes, headers, footers and after a column change (PF01). Since the preview's flow carries such a table itself (preview P17, 2026-11-15) `_table_carry_ok()` is true and this correction does not run. | T; M |
| PF26 | Reading order (kind `order`): a table kept whole (`page_break` none, no `hold`, not `inline`) that does not fit on what is left of the page goes alone to the next page in Hangul, and the headings and text after it move up before it (its paragraph's empty line stays; the heading before it stays at the page end). The preview's flow does the same (preview P18) and reports it (`page_report` `deferred`); `check` gives one `order` finding per table. Fix (profile key `tables.hold_whole`, `readable` on): every table kept whole that fits on a page gets `hold` (holdAnchorAndSO, 개체와 조판 부호를 항상 같은 쪽에 놓기): its paragraph, and a heading that keeps with it, go with it to the next page. Not on a table that may be cut (Hangul does not cut a held table) and not on one higher than a page (Hangul left empty pages, kh_hugeh); a table that fix cuts later loses the `hold` fix gave it. | M: kh_none, kh_hold, kh_huge, kh_hugeh; a real planning document (pages 3-4: 3 headings without their tables): `order` 11 -> 0, 11 pages as Hangul with hold; T |
| PF27 | A table that goes over a page with no header row (`table_head`) repeats its first row when that row looks like a header (a cell with a fill or bold text, check/readability `marked`): fix (profile key `tables.repeat_header`) sets `header_rows` 1 and `repeat_header`. Hangul repeats it, also with fixed row heights (kh_rep, kh_reph). | M: a real review document, pages 15-19 (5 tables, header row on every page in Hangul); T |
| PF28 | A footnote that goes on at the next page (`note`, fix `notes.move_paragraph`): the paragraph with the last note of the page starts the next page; when that note is in a row of a table cut at the page end, the table gets more room above it (as PF17 does for a short last part) so that the cut moves above that row. Kept only when the notes that go on become fewer and the page count grows by one at most. | M: a real review document (the note of row 9 of the review table went on: the row and its note now on the next page in Hangul); T |
